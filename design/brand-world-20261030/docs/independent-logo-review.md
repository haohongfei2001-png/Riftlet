# Riftlet — Independent logo review

Date: 2026-10-30. Reviewed actual generated images A / Split R, B / Passage and C / Inflection using image inspection, plus `product-truth.md`. No artwork or source was changed. The images are concepts, not final vector master files.

## Verdict

**Develop B / Passage as the primary candidate, retain A as a credible fallback, and stop C.** This is a conditional creative choice: B has the best system potential, but it has not yet earned a final-quality claim until its vector, monochrome and small-size versions are inspected.

The main advantage of B is relational. A substantial shape and one narrow vertical boundary can become a logo, a layout device and an interaction demonstration without inventing another product metaphor. That relationship is present in Riftlet's actual interface. It is not necessary for a first-time viewer to infer “ChatGPT screenshot utility” from the isolated mark; that is the job of the adjacent explanation and demonstrated behavior.

## B: problems that must be fixed in the vector

### 1. Make the boundary stable and less blade-like

The generated right component is tall, pointed and curved. That gives it a knife/leaf quality and makes the opening look organic rather than precise. Use a stable vertical terminal and a controlled, slightly softened taper. Do not add a decorative bend, bevel or asymmetric top hook simply because the generated image happened to contain it. The actual product boundary is stable, thin and vertical.

### 2. Reduce the deep scoop in the main shape

The left shape has a pronounced concave right side. The inward scoop is visually distinctive, but it can read as a loudspeaker or a shape being repelled from the line. It does not intrinsically communicate motion toward the line. Reduce the concavity and make its curvature deliberate; retain enough asymmetry to avoid becoming a generic rounded rectangle beside a bar.

Do not explain the generated form as a faithful diagram of the screenshot's resting shape. Actual resting thumbnails retain their original proportions. The mark can abstract a passage; the product demonstration should remain geometrically truthful.

### 3. Normalize the relationship before ornament

Begin with a one-color master with two components. Set the main object's width at roughly 70–75% of the full mark, the boundary at roughly 8–12%, and the narrowest visible gap around 7–10%. These are starting ranges for optical testing, not a rule to obey despite bad results. The current image's large negative-space scoop should not dictate all final scales.

Keep the boundary's extra height restrained. An oversized blade dominates the shape and looks less quiet. Aim for the two components to read as one relationship instead of separate pictograms.

### 4. Make small-size masters, not seven blind reductions

- At 16px, keep at least a visibly open approximately 1–1.5px gap after rasterization; a theoretical vector gap is insufficient. Favor a clear 1.5–2px boundary and simplify any taper.
- At 32px, preserve the same center of mass and open gap; remove shading from the symbol.
- At 64–128px, the normal mark should already work without material effects.
- At 256–1024px, refine curves, negative space and optical balance. Extra texture must not conceal a mediocre silhouette.
- Align on the actual pixel grid for small PNG/favicon exports and inspect at native display size on both light and dark backgrounds.

Only actual exported renders can confirm these targets. This reviewer has seen the large concept images, not those future exports.

### 5. Separate flat brand mark from App Icon material

The generated B App Icon has an attractive mint/porcelain combination, but the bright upper-left mint gives the pale symbol weak separation. The raised sticker texture is also stronger than the small, precise product suggests.

For the app icon, retain restrained depth in the background tile and a subtle cast separation, while keeping the symbol itself clear. A deeper teal background supports the warm off-white mark better. Do not put an internal squircle inside another export padding squircle. Keep controlled optical margins and export the complete 1024 master with transparent external corners if the chosen macOS asset workflow requires them.

For the website and menu bar, use a crisp single-color mark. Menu-bar variants should be template-compatible with no permanent mint tile, gradients or drop shadow.

### 6. Keep the wordmark calm and legally reproducible

The current generated wordmark is generic but serviceable. Do not add a second “rift” cut to every letter: the symbol already carries the spatial device. Use a verified-license font or genuinely constructed letterforms, and export an outlined wordmark alongside editable text specifications. Do not imply that AI-rendered glyphs establish the right to distribute an unknown font.

Check optical spacing around `Ri`, `ft` and `tl`, where verticals and short crossbars can create uneven texture. One careful weight and spacing system will be more mature than custom gimmicks.

## Motion use

Let the line stay still while the object approaches and becomes occluded. This teaches the logo's meaning without moving the whole identity. A brand demonstration may use slowed explanatory timing, clearly separate from the native 120ms disappearance specification.

Do not use a looping inhale/exhale effect suggesting continual surveillance or automatic collection. Do not make the mark permanently animate in the navbar. Respect reduced motion by showing the final state or using a simple dissolve.

## Similarity risk

No visual match to a named third-party logo has been established by this review. That is not a clearance claim. B's broad “object plus vertical line” family is common enough that web/image comparison is warranted before adoption. A has a higher generic-monogram risk, while C belongs to the very crowded folded-paper/sheet family.

Do not state “originality verified” or “no trademark conflict.” The appropriate current claim is “independently developed direction; preliminary visual comparison; not a legal clearance.”

## Product-truth implications for the website

The reviewed product evidence shows ordinary production attachment is still disabled, safe failure/retry admission is closed, and the supported attachment proof is restricted. Therefore:

- The site may show the intended flow under an explicit local-simulation label, with “internal testing” visible early.
- A failure-restoration demo must be labeled as conditional target behavior, not a feature already cleared for general release.
- The screenshot must not disappear on click. The simulated positive upload-start signal comes first; final attachment readiness follows later.
- The empty queue has no permanent edge trace. The logo can retain its boundary, but the product demonstration must not suggest the native edge stays visible without images.
- A mobile explanatory demo should not imply there is an iPhone version.

## Acceptance for recommending B

The final set should show the flat symbol beside the polished app icon, not let the beauty of the latter substitute for the former. Inspect 16/32px against light and dark backgrounds; verify exact geometry reused across assets; compare the logo with the actual site header; and test the main demonstration. If B loses its relationship at the smallest sizes, simplify that optical variant before revisiting the whole direction.
